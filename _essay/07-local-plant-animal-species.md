> *"Idaho has hundreds of square miles of forests as primitive and unfrequented as it was centuries ago, where big and small game live unmolested in native haunts - one of the last of our heritages of wildlife which has not been touched by the mania of destruction which characterizes modern America. So much of the state is still wild and inaccessible - much of its mountain wilderness is not penetrated by trails; and second, and perhaps the main reason, is that this region has not had publicity. It is with extreme reluctance that I give even this account of the (hunting) possibilities of this paradise, for once its potential possibilities are known there are hundreds of nimrods who stand ready to go even to the far end of our hemisphere if the game is really there."*
> — *Claude P. Fordyce (Associate Editor of Outdoor Life Magazine) published on June 25th, 1925 after a hunting trip with Dave Lewis along Big Creek [11].*

## Species

Although a list does not exist on every species in the Big Creek Watershed, extensive work has been done to catalog the plants and animals that live in that area.
