## Remote, But Not Forgotten

Although TWRS is very isolated and unknown by many people, it has received quite a bit of publicity over the past century. Ranging from newspaper clippings to full on National Geographic film documentaries. This media has thankfully not hurt the Taylor Wilderness Research Station, but has instead introduced people to the wonders of the Idaho wilderness.

## National Television

During his study on mountain lions, Dr. Maurice Hornocker and his crew began to draw national attention. This led to them becoming the subject of a National Geographic film documentary in 1973 and an appearance on ABC's Good Morning America in the winter of 1987 [8].

Most importantly, the research that was conducted resulted in the status of mountain lions in Idaho to change from a bounty animal to a big game species in 1972. This now meant that mountain lion hunting was restricted to only be during the designated seasons set by the Idaho Fish and Game and eventually required a hunting tag as well [8].

## Setting for Podcasts

As part of the Science Communication class at the University of Idaho's McCall Outdoor Science School, graduate students are taught how to communicate science both effectively and creatively. All students complete several assignments where they learn to make podcasts, videos, along with a variety of other creative avenues. Several students have gone on to create, be interviewed, and edit public podcasts of their own.

## Idaho Public Television

Outdoor Idaho is a half-hour program that has aired on Idaho Public Television since 1983. They cover a variety of topics and locations all shot in high definition and on location. The Taylor Wilderness Research Station, has been a highlight of several of these episodes, along with a full documentary. Each of these pieces show the uniqueness of Taylor and what it adds to the Frank Church - River of No Return Wilderness.

> *Outdoor Idaho explores the beauty, the drama and the science of nature, while examining the significant environmental and resource issues facing the west. - idahoptv.org [25].*

## Magazines

It was a century ago that the Taylor Wilderness Research Station first appeared in a magazine. After guiding several well known men through the Idaho mountains around his place, Dave Lewis was featured in a hunting article in the June 1925 issue of Outdoor Life Magazine.

---

The February 1970 issue of Field & Stream follows Dr. Maurice Hornocker and houndsmen Wilbur Wiles as they conduct research for the Idaho Fish and Game department, Univeristy of Idaho, and the University of British Columbia. The study was done over the course of five winters in a 200 square mile rugged area of Big Creek that is winter range for the deer and elk that are forced into the valley due to the deep snow that collects on the higher elevations [26].

In 1972, LIFE magazine published a series on marriage experiments. Joy and Stan Potts were highlighted as a couple who spent three months every year in the wilderness where they operated a commercial hunting camp, while leaving their children at their family alfalfa ranch in Nevada. Their outfitting business was run out of Taylor Ranch for several years until moving on to Cabin Creek. The article goes on to showcase how they have made their marriage flourish as they work together in the Idaho Wilderness [27].

Con and Tina Hourihan ran Taylor Ranch Outfitters where they would take clients fishing for cutthroat trout, whitefish, Dolly Varden, and rainbow trout. Big Creek served to be a good fishing location to catch all 4 salmonid species [28].
